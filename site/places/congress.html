How to weigh in on a congressional hearing

To weigh in on a congressional hearing, contact your representative and senators before it, or send a written statement for the hearing record where the committee accepts one. Witnesses who testify are invited by the committee.

Congressional hearings hear from invited witnesses. You weigh in by contacting your representative and senators, who sit on the committees; some committees also accept written statements from the public for the hearing record (each committee's site says how).

Official U.S. Congress legislature site ↗

Steps

  1. Find the hearing or markup for the bill or topic you care about (upcoming ones are listed below).
  2. Find your representative and senators: https://www.congress.gov/members/find-your-member
  3. Call, email or write to them before the hearing, naming the bill number and your position.
  4. If the committee accepts statements from the public for the record, send yours by its deadline (each committee's site says how).

Tips

  • Name the bill number (or the rule's docket number) so your input reaches the right item.
  • Say whether you support or oppose it, and why, in a few sentences from your own experience.
  • Send it before the deadline on the hearing notice: many forms close when the hearing starts.
  • What you submit is usually public record, including your name.
